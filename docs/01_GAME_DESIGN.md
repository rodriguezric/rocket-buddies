# Game Design

## High Concept
Rocket Buddies is a 2D space adventure made of interconnected gameplay modes. Players accept missions aboard their rocket, prepare supplies, travel to destinations, explore, solve problems, collect discoveries, rescue Meeps, deal with mischievous Merfs and return home with rewards.

## Audience
Primary: roughly ages 6–10, playable with limited reading support.
Secondary: parents and children playing together.

Avoid requiring fast reflexes for progression. Reading should be concise, supported by icons and optional narration-ready text.

## Core Loop
1. Visit the mission board.
2. Pick a destination/mission.
3. Prepare rocket fuel and optional equipment.
4. Launch.
5. Play Rocket Flight.
6. Arrive at a destination.
7. Explore.
8. Trigger activities: discoveries, cheese hunts, Meep rescues, Merf Mischief, environmental puzzles.
9. Complete the mission objective.
10. Return to the rocket.
11. Fly home or continue if fuel permits.
12. Receive Stars/discoveries/resources.
13. Upgrade/decorate/unlock.
14. Choose another adventure.

## Session Structure
A small mission should target about 5–10 minutes. Larger missions can chain several activities for 15–25 minute adventures. A child should be able to stop safely after returning to the rocket.

## Full Game Metagame
The rocket is the persistent hub. It contains:
- Cockpit / Solar System Map
- Mission Board
- Meep Habitat
- Science Journal
- Workshop / Upgrades
- Cheese Vault
- Cosmetic/decorating spaces

## Progression
Use several orthogonal forms of progress:
- **Stars** — broad completion currency.
- **Discoveries** — journal completion and destination knowledge.
- **Meeps** — collectible characters with names/personality.
- **Rocket upgrades** — expand possibilities rather than simply raising stats.
- **Cosmetics** — suits, rocket decorations, Meep habitat items.
- **Destinations** — unlocked through missions and rocket capability.

Avoid grind and daily-login mechanics.

## Failure Philosophy
Failure should generally mean:
- lose a little time,
- consume a small resource,
- retry immediately,
- receive a humorous animation,
- or find an alternate solution.

Never permanently lose a rescued Meep. Do not make learning mistakes embarrassing.

## Tone
Warm, curious, silly and optimistic.

Merfs are antagonistic troublemakers, not frightening monsters. They steal cheese, block paths, make ridiculous machines and create problems. Some encounters can reveal that a Merf was misunderstood.

## World Rules
- Real astronomy is treated accurately when practical.
- Moon Cheese is impossible and nevertheless unquestionably exists.
- Meeps love Moon Cheese.
- Merfs also love Moon Cheese and frequently steal it.
- Nobody can satisfactorily explain the Moon Cheese.
- Players cannot casually land on gas giants; their moons and orbital environments become destinations instead.

## Accessibility
Plan from the start for:
- remappable controls,
- controller/touch/keyboard,
- adjustable text speed,
- low-reading presentation,
- reduced motion,
- generous timing,
- no color-only critical information,
- optional hints.
